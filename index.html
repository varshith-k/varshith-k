Hurricane Week

An interactive hurricane-preparedness simulator: you're a Miami-Dade resident with $400 and five days before landfall, and every decision — evacuate or shelter, insure or risk it — changes your financial and safety outcome. A deterministic rules engine drives every consequence; an AI coach (Gemini, grounded in your actual choices) explains the outcome afterward instead of deciding it.

  • Grounded Readiness Assistant answers real prep questions from verified FEMA/NOAA facts via Snowflake Cortex RAG, not generic LLM guesses
  • Replay mode diffs your new run against your last one and explains which decisions moved the score
  • An explicit AI-transparency panel discloses exactly what each AI feature receives — no account or identity data ever leaves the system
  • Full stack: Next.js 16 + FastAPI, with MongoDB Atlas, TimescaleDB (Tiger Data), Snowflake, Gemini, ElevenLabs, and a Solana devnet completion record

Conversational Search Engine

Copart Conversational Search

Natural-language search and multi-turn refinement over ~26,000 real Copart auction lots. Ask for what you want in plain English, change your mind mid-conversation, and always see exactly what the system currently believes you asked for — backed by a deterministic, rule-based filter extractor with an optional LLM extractor behind the same interface.

  • Runs with zero API key: default extraction is deterministic and fully offline-testable
  • Surfaces a real data-coverage gap instead of hiding it — Copart's public export withholds price data, so the app detects that from the data itself and tells the user, rather than estimating a number
  • Structured filtering with deterministic SQL retrieval and explicit conversational state across turns

02 — About

From IIIT Sri City to production
systems at Couture AI, now at UF.

I started out building full-stack class projects at IIIT Sri City, graduated into nearly three years as a software engineer at Couture AI, and I'm now back in school at the University of Florida to go deeper on the systems side — distributed systems, ML, and the theory behind the tools I'd already been shipping with in production.

Most of what I build sits at the intersection of backend engineering and applied AI: services that have to stay correct and fast under real load, with an LLM or retrieval layer bolted on in a way that's observable instead of magical. I like projects that are honest about their own limitations — Copart Conversational Search tells you outright when a filter can't match anything instead of faking a result, and that's the kind of engineering judgment I try to bring everywhere.

Outside of coursework and internships, I build weekend and hackathon projects (Hurricane Week, built for ShellHacks/MLH) because shipping something end-to-end in a weekend is still the fastest way I know to learn a new piece of infrastructure.
